The most common worry on a switch is that the subscriptions have to be cancelled and bought again. They do not. Microsoft's Cloud Solution Provider (CSP) programme has a partner-to-partner transfer that moves billing ownership of existing subscriptions from the current partner to a new one. Here is what it does, and what it does not, with the Microsoft Learn page behind each point.

## Who can run one

Transfers are created by the new partner and approved by the current one. The customer does not initiate it. Both direct-bill partners and indirect providers (distributors) can send and accept transfer requests; an indirect reseller, which is what most MSPs are, needs its provider to do it on its behalf.

MSP Hub is a direct-bill partner, so it creates and runs the transfer itself.

Source: [Transfer billing ownership from one partner to another](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/transfer-subscriptions) on Microsoft Learn.

## Both partners must agree

Microsoft will not override a transfer without the current partner's approval, and the current partner is not obliged to accept. If they decline or simply do not respond, the request expires after 30 days. In practice this means the conversation with your current partner happens before the request is sent, not after.

Source: [Transfer billing ownership from one partner to another](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/transfer-subscriptions) on Microsoft Learn.

## What moves with the subscription

- **The price.** The transferred subscription is created under the new partner at the original subscription's price.
- **Promotions.** Promotions applied to the original subscription carry over.
- **Scheduled changes.** Renewal instructions and other scheduled changes transfer with it.
- **The term.** Transfers happen mid-term; the current partner is responsible up to the transfer date and the new partner for the remainder. There is no new cancellation window, because a transferred subscription is not a new term.

Source: [Transfer new commerce license-based subscriptions](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/transfer-new-commerce-license-based-subscriptions) on Microsoft Learn.

## What to plan for

- **Batches.** License-based transfers are limited to 25 line items per billing plan per request. A customer with 32 monthly subscriptions needs two requests; one with 20 monthly and 12 annual needs one. The deal desk sequences the batches.
- **Azure.** Azure subscriptions, reservations, and savings plans under an Azure plan transfer too, through a tool only partners with a direct-billing relationship with Microsoft can access. Azure CSP pricing under the current partner does not carry over, and role-based access assignments stay where they are.
- **Timing.** Once accepted, most transfers complete in under an hour. Microsoft allows up to 72 hours while any failed line items are retried.

Sources: [license-based transfer limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/transfer-new-commerce-license-based-subscriptions#transfer-limits) and [Azure plan transfers](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/transfer-azure-subscriptions-under-azure-plan) on Microsoft Learn.

## What the customer actually does

Three things: accept the reseller relationship with the new partner from a link, sign the Microsoft Customer Agreement with them, and tell the current partner the transfer is coming. Users keep working throughout; the tenant, the data, and the licences assigned to people do not change.
