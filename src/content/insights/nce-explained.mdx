Every Microsoft licensing quote eventually produces the same four questions. The answers come from Microsoft's New Commerce Experience (NCE) rules for license-based subscriptions, and they are the same whoever you buy from. Here they are, with the Microsoft Learn page each one comes from.

## 1. You have seven days to change your mind

A new license-based subscription can be cancelled with a prorated refund within the first seven days (168 hours) of its term. After that window closes, cancellation is no longer available and the subscription is billed for the full term, whichever billing plan you chose.

Perpetual software bought through CSP is different: it is refundable in full within 30 calendar days of purchase.

Source: [New Commerce Experience cancellation policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/new-commerce-cancellation-policy) and [cancel a subscription](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/create-a-new-subscription#cancel-a-subscription) on Microsoft Learn.

## 2. Seats go up any time; they come down within seven days

Adding seats is always possible. Reducing them is only possible within the first seven days of when those seats were added, and the rule applies whether they were added at the initial purchase, at renewal, or mid-term. Outside that window the seat count cannot be decreased until the next renewal.

Practical consequence: buy the seats you are sure of, add the rest when the people start, and keep the seven-day clock in mind when a project ends.

Source: [Increase or decrease licenses in new commerce subscriptions](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/create-a-new-subscription#increase-or-decrease-licenses-in-new-commerce-license-based-subscriptions) on Microsoft Learn.

## 3. Three terms, several billing plans

License-based subscriptions are sold on monthly, annual, and three-year terms where Microsoft offers them for the product. Each term has its own billing frequency options: an annual term, for example, can be billed up front or monthly. Monthly terms suit seats that change; annual and three-year terms lower the unit price for stable workloads. A good estate mixes them by user group.

Source: [Create customer subscriptions in Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/create-a-new-subscription) on Microsoft Learn.

## 4. Mid-term, you can only move to a longer term

A subscription can move to a longer term mid-way through: monthly to annual or three-year, annual to three-year. It cannot move to a shorter one until renewal, and changing only the billing frequency mid-term is not supported. Any change of term is also a change you can schedule for the renewal date instead.

One detail that catches people: a mid-term change of term does not reopen the seven-day cancellation window, and seat reductions stay unavailable until the renewal date.

Source: [Manage term duration and billing frequency](https://learn.microsoft.com/partner-center/customers/billing-frequency-changes) on Microsoft Learn.
