Microsoft 365 estates grow by accretion. A plan chosen for a pilot becomes the default. An add-on bought for one team is still billed three years later. Renewals auto-renew because nobody owns the date. The result is an estate that costs what it costs and delivers less than it could.

A licensing review fixes that in four moves.

## 1. Right-size the plan mix

Business Premium, Microsoft 365 E3, and E5 each bundle different security, compliance, and voice capabilities. Two patterns come up in almost every review:

- **E3 to E5 consolidation.** Organizations paying for E3 plus separate security and compliance add-ons often get more capability, and simpler administration, by consolidating onto E5.
- **Business Premium right-sizing.** Organizations under the Business plan seat limit frequently hold enterprise plans they do not need, or Business Standard seats that are missing the security they do need. Business Premium is often the better fit either way.

The review is per user group, not per company: frontline, knowledge workers, and admins rarely want the same plan.

## 2. Clean up add-ons and plan Copilot and Teams Phone deliberately

Add-ons bought for projects that ended, duplicate security tools, and unused calling plans are the easiest savings in the estate. Copilot for Microsoft 365 and Teams Phone are the two additions most organizations are now planning; both deserve a pilot group, a success measure, and a term decision before they roll out widely.

## 3. Choose terms on purpose

Under the New Commerce Experience (NCE), license-based subscriptions can be bought monthly, annually, or on three-year terms where Microsoft offers them. Monthly terms suit seats that change; annual and triennial terms lower the unit price for stable workloads. A good estate mixes them.
